# Turath Collective — Marketing & Content Strategy

*For a solo founder with an iPhone, no team, and no ad-agency budget. Written 2026-07.*

Everything here respects two hard rules:

1. **No music, ever.** Not royalty-free, not "just a little background." All audio is ambient/
   natural sound, silence with text overlay, or spoken-word voiceover with no instrumental bed.
2. **No founder-on-camera, no talking-head video.**

And one framework that governs every caption and claim:

> **Two-tier honesty.** Some pieces are *historical craft, historical context* (Hebron ceramics —
> a real workshop tradition in a real place). Others are *traditional technique, new object*
> (the hand-blown glass pomegranate). Say which is which. Never imply an object is old because
> the technique is. Never invent reviews, followers, or "as seen in."

---

## 1. Content pillars

### Pillar 1 — Object photography (the backbone, ~60% of posts)
Static, styled shots per the design guide: warm raw wood surface, dramatic low golden-hour side
light, long soft shadows, **one** organic prop maximum (a dried citrus slice, a sprig, a fig —
never a table setting, never plates/cutlery in use). The object is treated as art, not tableware.

- Works as: IG feed posts, IG Stories (re-crops), TikTok photo-mode carousels, ad creative.
- Each shoot session should bank 6–10 usable frames (angles, distances, crops) so one afternoon
  of shooting feeds 2–3 weeks of posting.

### Pillar 2 — Process / making-of footage (the differentiator, ~25%)
Fable can't film in a Hebron glass workshop. You can. Real access to Hebron and Jabaa craftsmen
is the one asset competitors cannot buy — molten glass turning, a wheel spinning, glaze being
poured, hands doing something for the ten-thousandth time.

- Format: 20–45s clips, **ambient workshop sound only** (the roar of the furnace, the wheel, the
  brush — this sound is genuinely better content than any track). Optional sparse text overlay
  ("Hebron. The same furnaces, four generations.") or a quiet voiceover recorded on the iPhone.
- These come from supplier visits or footage the artisans/partners send you. Ask for vertical
  video, 30–60s, steady, close on hands. Even mediocre real footage outperforms polished stock.
- Label it truthfully: name the city and craft; don't claim a specific artisan made *your* SKU
  unless that's literally true.

### Pillar 3 — Object-in-context lifestyle (~15%)
The piece living in a quiet, real home corner: pomegranate on a stack of books at dusk, bowl on
a windowsill. Same lighting rules. No people's faces needed (hands are fine). This pillar sells
the *feeling*; keep it sparse and unstaged-looking.

**Recurring micro-format for all three pillars:** extreme close-up texture shots (glaze
crackle, glass bubbles, brush strokes). Cheap to make, high save-rate, perfect Stories filler.

## 2. iPhone shooting guide (the golden-hour editorial look, no gear)

**Light**
- Shoot within ~90 minutes of sunset (or just after sunrise) next to one window; sun low and
  raking sideways across the surface. Kill every other light in the room — mixed light is what
  makes photos look amateur.
- Cloudy day / wrong hour: place a cheap warm-white LED panel or even a table lamp low and to
  the side, far from the object, and shoot in the resulting pool of light. Golden hour is a
  *look* (warm + low + directional), not only a time.
- Long shadows are the aesthetic. Don't fill them. If the shadow side goes too black, bounce a
  little light back with a white foam board.

**Camera settings**
- Clean the lens (genuinely the #1 fix). Shoot 4K/30 for video, HEIC/ProRAW stills if available.
- Tap the object to focus, then **drag exposure down** until highlights glow and shadows deepen.
  Underexposed-by-a-third is the editorial look; the phone's auto brightening is the enemy.
- Use 2× or 3× lens for objects (the 1× wide distorts close subjects). Never digital-zoom past
  the optical steps. Portrait mode sparingly — its fake blur smears glass edges.
- Lock white balance warm if your iPhone model allows it, or warm slightly in the built-in
  editor (+10–15 warmth). All photos get the *same* edit — consistency reads as brand.

**Set**
- One board of real wood (a scaffold plank or cutting board from a reuse store, ~$0–15) beats
  any backdrop. Optionally a second, darker board as a background.
- One prop per shot, or none.

**Worth buying (≈$50 total)** — cheap tripod with phone mount (~$20–25, mandatory for video and
repeatable framing), 5-in-1 collapsible reflector ~60cm (~$15, use the white side), white foam
board (~$5). **Skip**: ring lights (flat, cold, the opposite of this brand), lens kits, gimbals
(tripod + slow pans cover it), softboxes (the window is the softbox).

**Video specifics**: tripod always; move the *object* or your hands, not the camera; 0.5× slow
motion on pours/rotation reads as luxury; record 10s of clean ambient sound before/after each
clip for editing headroom.

## 3. AI-generated imagery — where it's allowed, where it must end

**Acceptable as a temporary stand-in (pre-samples only):**
- Mood/concept exploration and testing feed aesthetics before product samples exist.
- Website hero/lifestyle placeholders while waiting on real photography.
- Low-spend *concept* test ads to compare styling directions — acceptable only while no physical
  product photos exist, and retire them the moment real samples arrive.

**Never, at any stage:**
- AI images passed off as a real artisan, workshop, customer, or testimonial.
- AI "process" footage implying you filmed in Hebron/Jabaa when you didn't — this pillar's whole
  value is that it's real; faking it once poisons it permanently.
- AI product images once real samples exist (an AI bowl misrepresents glaze, weight, color —
  that's a returns problem, not just an ethics problem).

**Hard gate before launch/scaling ads:** every product photo a customer can buy from must be a
real photo of the real product. Replace AI heroes on the site before the first real ad dollar
beyond the concept test. When in doubt: could you honestly answer "is this photo real?" from a
customer DM? If the answer needs a paragraph, don't post it.

## 4. Posting cadence (sustainable for one person)

| What | How often | Effort source |
|---|---|---|
| Static object/lifestyle post (IG feed, cross-post TikTok photo mode) | 2–3 / week | One golden-hour shoot every 2–3 weeks banks all of it |
| Process video (Reel + TikTok) | 1 every 1–2 weeks | Supplier footage + your visits; batch-edit |
| Stories (texture close-ups, work-in-progress, restock notes) | 2–3 light touches / week | Shot leftovers; zero new production |

Rules that keep this honest: batch everything (shoot once, schedule 2–3 weeks via Meta Business
Suite — free); never break the visual rules to hit a date — skipping a post costs nothing, an
off-brand post costs positioning; captions are 1–3 quiet sentences (place, craft, material),
no hashtag walls (3–5 specific tags), no exclamation marks, no "flash sale" language ever.

Time budget: roughly one afternoon shoot every 2–3 weeks + 2–3 hrs/week editing, captioning,
scheduling, replying. If a week collapses, Stories only — the feed can breathe.

## 5. Launch sequencing — validate before inventory

**Phase 0 — Foundation (now):** finish site honesty items (real legal copy, cookie consent,
working analytics funnel — PostHog `$pageview → add_to_cart → begin_checkout` is already built),
bank 3–4 weeks of content, remove the Shopify store password when ready to sell.

**Phase 1 — Organic warm-up (2–4 weeks):** post on cadence; watch saves/shares/profile-visits
(quality signals), not follower count. Goal is a feed that looks alive before strangers from
ads land on it.

**Phase 2 — Test ads (small, e.g. $10–20/day for 2–3 weeks):** run 2–3 creatives (best static
object shot vs. best process clip) to cold audiences, IG/FB placements via Meta Ads Manager.
Judge on the PostHog funnel, per creative:
- **CTR** on the ad (≥ ~1% is a workable signal for cold traffic),
- **product-page → add_to_cart** (healthy e-comm lands ~5–10%),
- **add_to_cart → begin_checkout** (~30–50%),
- actual purchases matter less at this spend than *shape* of the funnel — you're buying
  information, not revenue.

**Scale signals (any two):** one creative consistently beating the others on CTR *and* carrying
through to add_to_cart; cost per add_to_cart that implies plausible unit economics at your
margins; organic saves/DMs asking "when can I buy."
**Pull-back signals:** clicks but near-zero add_to_cart (price/product-page problem — fix before
spending more); high add_to_cart but checkout abandonment (shipping-cost shock — revisit
threshold/rates); CTR under ~0.5% across all creatives (the creative or the audience is wrong —
back to Phase 1, don't out-spend a creative problem).

Only after the funnel holds at test spend do you commit to real inventory depth, then scale
spend gradually (≤ ~20–30%/week so learning survives).

## 6. Platform notes

**Instagram (primary — the brand's natural home)**
- Feed = pillars 1 & 3 (the permanent gallery; buyers *will* scroll it before purchasing — it's
  the second storefront). Reels = pillar 2 process clips; ambient sound is fine and even an
  advantage ("original audio" is normal for craft content — never tap "add music").
- Stories = low-stakes texture/behind-scenes; Highlights: "Hebron", "The Craft", "Care".
- Grid discipline: warm wood tones dominate; if a shot fights the grid, don't post it.

**TikTok (secondary — process content only)**
- Pillar 2 clips cross-post well: craft/process TikTok is a huge niche where ambient-sound,
  no-face videos are native, and text-overlay storytelling performs. Slightly rawer edits than
  IG are fine; photo-mode carousels of statics work as filler.
- Don't chase trends, sounds, or challenges — they're all music-based and off-brand anyway.
  Accept slower growth for coherence.

**Skip for now:** Pinterest (revisit post-launch — good long-tail fit but another queue to feed),
Facebook page (exists as ad infrastructure; auto-cross-post at most), and any platform requiring
founder-on-camera talking content by design.

---

## 7. One-line operating principle

Post less, never off-brand, let the real workshops carry the story, spend money only after the
funnel proves strangers want the thing — and never let a single fake element (review, follower,
AI artisan) into a brand whose entire premise is authenticity.
