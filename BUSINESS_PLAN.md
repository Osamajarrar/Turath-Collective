## 1. Executive summary

Turath Collective sells handcrafted heritage homeware made by real artisans, for people who
want to get away from mass-produced, soulless objects. It launches with Palestinian
craftsmanship — Hebron ceramics, Hebron/Phoenician-tradition glass, and a signature hand-blown
glass pomegranate — because that is where the founder has genuine access and knowledge, not
because the brand is permanently Palestine-only. The long-term vision is heritage crafts
globally, wherever endangered, historically significant craftsmanship is still practiced.

- **Tagline:** "History, still handmade."
- **Positioning model:** Apple, not Gucci/LV — premium, not exclusive. No drop culture, no
  artificial scarcity, no invite-only framing.
- **Customer:** global, story-and-craftsmanship-driven; skews Western, 25–35, higher
  disposable income initially — explicitly *not* diaspora- or cause-marketing-driven.
- **Founding constraint that shapes everything:** ~$2,000 CAD starting capital, one founder,
  Montreal-based. The plan below is built around validating demand *before* committing to
  inventory, using samples, organic content, and small test-ad spend.
- **Non-negotiable operating principle:** no lying, ever — a religious commitment, not a brand
  aesthetic. No fake urgency, invented reviews, or unverifiable claims. This is also a genuine
  competitive position: the craft/heritage e-commerce space is full of vague provenance claims,
  and a brand that is precise about what is old (the craft) and what is new (some objects) is
  rare.

**Where the business is today:** development-stage. Website built (headless Shopify, EN/FR),
store not yet live (dev store, storefront password, test payments), supplier decision open
(two glass sources being compared via physical samples), pricing not yet set, no revenue.

**The next 90 days, in one line:** get samples in hand → shoot real content → finish the launch
checklist → run small paid tests → only then order inventory at depth.

## 3. Products & sourcing

### 3.1 The two-tier honesty framework (governs all product claims)

Every product is classified as one of:

1. **Historical object + historical craft** — the object form itself is centuries old
   (e.g. Hebron ceramics: traditional forms, traditional decoration, made the traditional way).
   Copy may speak about the age of both the craft *and* the object tradition.
2. **Traditional craft + new object** — the technique is old and rare; the object is a modern
   application of it (e.g. the glass pomegranate). Copy may celebrate the technique's history
   but must never imply the object form is ancient.

"Phoenician glass" as a category name is verified accurate at the level of technique lineage
(Phoenician glassblowers pioneered free-blowing; the tradition migrated to and survives in
Hebron). The swirled-marble aesthetic is a Hebron-era stylistic development, not a literal
ancient decorative pattern — product copy must be precise about which claim is being made.

### 3.2 Launch catalog (intended)

| Product line | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Hebron ceramics — classic blue | 1 (old object, old craft) | Supplier identified. Custom color matching explored and found infeasible with current manufacturer; classic blue only. A second ceramics supplier contact is in progress in parallel. |
| Hand-blown glass (vessels/objects) | Depends on piece | **Open supplier decision — see 3.3.** |
| Glass pomegranate (signature/hero piece) | 2 (new object, old technique) | Made by Mohammad Twam (Jaba'). Real cultural weight — the pomegranate is a Palestinian/Levantine symbol of abundance and homeland — but the object form is contemporary and copy must say so. Hero piece, not a full line. |

The pomegranate's role: the brand's visual signature and the anchor of the process-content
strategy (a rare raw-glass artisan is the story competitors cannot copy). It is not expected
to carry revenue alone.

### 3.3 The open glass-supplier decision (do not resolve on paper — resolve with samples)

| Factor | Mohammad Twam (Jaba') | Hebron glassmakers |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Raw glass, oxygen-fired | Recycled glass |
| Color consistency | Tighter | More batch variance |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Availability elsewhere | Rare — hard to find from other sellers | Commonly available from other sellers already |
| Story exclusivity | High (one of few raw-glass artisans) | Shared with existing competitors |
| Supply risk | Concentrated in one artisan | Multiple workshops |

**Decision method (already in motion):** physical samples from both are being ordered. The
comparison will be settled by a real side-by-side test — including photographing both for
test-ad creative and letting funnel data (CTR, add-to-cart) speak — not by a subjective call.
The honest possibility that this is not either/or should stay open: Twam as premium/signature
tier and Hebron glass as accessible tier is a plausible outcome *if* margins support both.

**What this decision blocks:** final pricing (3.4), packaging spec, and inventory budget.

### 3.4 Pricing — not yet set (`[needs founder input]`, blocks several things)

No pricing strategy is finalized. Deliberately not inventing numbers here. What pricing must
account for when it is set:

- Landed cost per unit: artisan price + international shipping + duties/brokerage + damage
  allowance (fragile goods *will* break in transit at some rate — treat breakage as a cost of
  goods, not a surprise; the rate is unknown until first shipments: `[needs data]`).
- Packaging cost per unit (protective + brand-worthy; see 5.3).
- Payment processing (~2.9% + fixed fee is typical — confirm current Shopify Payments rates).
- Ad cost tolerance: at small scale, contribution margin per unit must plausibly cover cost
  per acquisition seen in test ads, or the model doesn't close.
- Positioning: premium-not-exclusive argues for "expensive enough to signal quality and pay
  artisans properly, not so high it becomes a luxury-scarcity game."

**Blocked until pricing exists:** free-shipping threshold (`VITE_FREE_SHIPPING_THRESHOLD`),
announcement bar (`VITE_SHOW_SHIPPING_PROMO` stays off), margin math, and the pre-order
decision (6.4).

## 4. Market & target customer

### 4.1 Target customer (fixed positioning — from CLAUDE.md)

Global, story-and-craftsmanship-driven. Skews Western, 25–35, higher disposable income
initially, but not exclusive to that group. **Not** targeted at diaspora identity or
cause/solidarity purchasing — no "support Palestine" framing, no donation-style messaging.
The purchase motivation being sold to: *"I want real objects with real history in my home,
made by someone whose skill I can see."* This targeting holds as the brand expands beyond
Palestine — don't retarget per-origin.

### 4.2 Market context — what is known vs. what needs research

Known qualitatively (observable, no numbers claimed):

- A broad consumer movement away from mass-produced homeware toward artisanal/handmade exists
  (visible in the growth of craft marketplaces, "slow living" content, and premium DTC
  homeware brands).
- Craft/process video content ("making-of") is a proven high-engagement niche on
  TikTok/Instagram, and ambient-sound no-face video is native to it — which happens to match
  this brand's hard constraints exactly.
- Existing sellers of Hebron glass/ceramics are mostly either souvenir-grade storefronts,
  solidarity-framed shops, or Etsy listings — few present the work as premium design objects
  with editorial standards. *(This is an observation from informal browsing, not a market
  study.)*

**`[needs research]` — real market research to do before scaling spend (not before testing;
the $10–20/day test phase itself is the cheapest market research available):**

- Named competitor audit: who sells Palestinian/Levantine glass & ceramics online, at what
  prices, with what shipping promises and what framing. (Do not rely on the informal
  impression above when setting price.)
- Price benchmarking against adjacent premium craft brands (Japanese ceramics DTC, Moroccan
  zellige/homeware brands, etc.) to locate the credible premium band.
- Size-of-market numbers, if ever needed for financing conversations — source them from real
  reports and cite them; none are asserted here.

### 4.3 Differentiation (honest version)

1. **Access:** direct relationships with working artisans in Hebron and Jaba', including one
   of very few raw-glass artisans — real process footage competitors cannot buy.
2. **Editorial standard:** golden-hour, art-object presentation vs. souvenir-grade listings.
3. **Radical honesty as positioning:** precise provenance claims in a category soaked in vague
   ones. This is verifiable by customers and compounds as trust.
4. **Not cause-framed:** the work competes on craft and beauty, which widens the market beyond
   sympathy purchases and ages better.

## 5. Operations & supply chain

### 5.1 Sourcing model — small-batch by necessity and by design

With $2k capital there is no version of this that starts with a container. The model:

1. **Samples first** (in motion): both glass sources + ceramics. Samples serve triple duty:
   QC evaluation, photography/content assets, and test-ad creative.
2. **First real order = small batch**, sized by what test-ad data supports and what capital
   allows after fixed costs — tens of units, not hundreds. `[exact quantity: after pricing +
   test data]`
3. **Reorder cadence over depth:** smaller, more frequent orders trade shipping efficiency for
   capital safety and demand-matching. Accept the worse per-unit freight economics at first;
   revisit as volume grows.

### 5.2 Inbound logistics — `[needs research before first order]`

- Shipping route and mode from the West Bank to Montreal (courier vs. freight forwarder),
  realistic transit times, and cost per kg — get real quotes, do not assume.
- Customs: tariff classification for hand-made ceramics/glassware, applicable tariff
  treatment, brokerage fees, CARM/importer registration (see §2).
- Fragility: insist on double-boxing and test the supplier's export packing with the sample
  shipments — the samples' arrival condition is data.
- Political/security disruption risk on the origin side is real and outside anyone's control;
  see §10. Mitigation is buffer stock and honest customer communication, not pretending
  schedules are guaranteed.

### 5.3 Quality control & packaging

- **QC:** photo-based pre-shipment checks from suppliers + 100% inspection on arrival (feasible
  at small batch sizes; define per-product accept/reject criteria after samples arrive —
  glass: bubbles/weight/color-band tolerance; ceramics: glaze defects, chips, paint quality).
  Batch variance in recycled glass is *presentable as character* only within honest limits —
  photograph representative pieces, and say "each piece varies" because it's true.
- **Packaging:** dual requirement — survives international shipping (fragile), and unboxing
  matches a premium brand. Cost per unit unknown until pricing/volume set:
  `[needs quotes — decide after pricing strategy, since packaging spend must fit margin]`.
  Start simple and honest (sturdy box, paper fill, one printed card telling the true story of
  the piece and its maker) rather than luxury packaging the margin can't carry.
- **Food-safety compliance (ceramics) — important, `[needs verification]`:** if ceramics are
  sold as tableware (not purely decorative), Canada regulates leachable lead/cadmium in glazed
  ceramics. Either obtain compliant lab results, or explicitly sell and label as decorative.
  Do not skip this — it is exactly the kind of claim (food-safe or not) the honesty rules
  require to be verified, and it has legal force.

### 5.4 Fulfillment & customer service

- **Fulfillment from the founder's home in Montreal** at MVP scale: pack-and-ship per order
  via Canada Post/courier. This is the right answer until volume makes it painful; no 3PL at
  MVP.
- **Shipping zones:** Canada + US first; international selectively (fragile + far = expensive;
  quote real rates in Shopify rather than promising flat rates that lose money).
- **Returns/damage policy:** structure already drafted on the site's policy pages — keep the
  established pattern: no invented timelines; every number on the policy page must match
  Shopify's actual processing reality (the repo's claims-audit history documents exactly how
  this went wrong before). Damage-in-transit: photo-based claim, replacement or refund —
  breakage allowance is priced into COGS (5.2, 3.4).
- **Customer service:** founder-handled email. The site's contact form is currently
  disconnected from any backend — it must be wired (or removed) before launch so no promise
  of response is fake (checklist §2).

  confident wrong inventory bet.

it lands; the tranche gating is the point, not the placeholder amounts.*

### 6.3 MVP budget definition

"MVP" = the minimum to get the *first real order into a customer's hands honestly*: live
Shopify store (password off, real payments), real product photos, legal/policy pages with
verified numbers, one shippable product batch, packaging that survives transit. Everything
else (AR locale, accounts, newsletter backend, wholesale catalog) is explicitly deferred and
already scaffolded as such in the codebase.

### 6.4 Pre-orders as a funding mechanism — honest analysis (decision open)

**For:** converts demand signal into capital before inventory risk; fits small-batch craft
narrative truthfully ("made for you in this batch"); Shopify supports it natively.

**Against / constraints:**

- Honesty constraint is binding: a pre-order is a *promise with a date*. Given real
  West-Bank-origin logistics uncertainty, any promised window must be wide and padded, and
  breaking it requires proactive refunds/communication. If a truthful delivery window is so
  wide it kills conversion, pre-orders fail on their own terms.
- Consumer-protection rules and payment-processor policies constrain charging long before
  fulfillment `[needs research: current Shopify Payments rules on pre-order capture timing +
  Quebec consumer law on delivery obligations]`.
- Operationally it inverts QC: money taken before goods inspected. Mitigation: pre-orders only
  after samples have validated the supplier's quality and export packing.

**Recommendation (founder's call):** treat pre-orders as a *Phase 3 option*, unlocked only if
(a) test-ad funnel is strong, (b) a supplier has passed sample QC, and (c) a truthful delivery
window is known from the sample shipments' actual transit times. Alternative that raises no
promises: a "notify me" list (the site already has notify-me UI groundwork) used to gauge depth
of demand before ordering — weaker signal than money, zero honesty risk.

### 6.5 Unit economics template (fill when pricing lands)

```
Retail price (CAD)                     [set in 3.4]
– Artisan cost                          [per supplier quote]
– Inbound freight + duty (per unit)     [per broker quote / actual]
– Breakage allowance (% of COGS)        [from first shipments' data]
– Packaging                             [per quote]
– Payment processing                    [~2.9% + fee; verify]
– Outbound shipping subsidy (if any)    [depends on threshold decision]
= Contribution margin                   → must exceed observed CPA at test scale
```

### 6.6 Phased scaling path (capital strategy beyond $2k)

1. **Self-funded loop (now):** revenue from first batches recycles into slightly larger
   batches. Growth rate capped by cash conversion, and that's acceptable.
2. **Pre-orders (conditional, 6.4)** smooth the cash gap per batch.
3. **Only if the model is proven** (repeatable CPA < margin, low return rates): consider
   external capital — small business loan/BDC, or friends-and-family — to fund inventory
   depth and a second craft origin. No equity conversation makes sense before then.
4. **Scalability note:** the supply side is the real ceiling, not demand — artisan capacity
   is finite and *should* be (it's the product). Scaling = adding *crafts and origins*
   (the "Collective" in the name), not squeezing more units from the same aging hands.
   That expansion logic is also the wholesale prerequisite (§9).

## 7. Marketing approach

Fully specified in `MARKETING_STRATEGY.md`; this section only anchors the business-plan view.
Hard constraints: **no music in any content, ever** (religious constraint, no workarounds);
**no founder-on-camera**; two-tier honesty governs every caption.

- **Three content pillars:** object photography (~60%), process/making-of footage (~25% — the
  differentiator; real workshop access is the moat), object-in-context lifestyle (~15%).
  iPhone + ~$50 of gear; golden-hour editorial look per the design guide.
- **Channels:** Instagram primary, TikTok secondary (process content is native there and
  ambient-sound/no-face is normal in the craft niche — the constraints cost nothing).
  Pinterest/Facebook deferred.
- **Sequencing:** organic warm-up (2–4 wks) → $10–20/day Meta test ads (2–3 wks) judged on the
  PostHog funnel (pageview → add_to_cart → begin_checkout), with explicit scale and pull-back
  signals already defined in MARKETING_STRATEGY.md §5.
- **AI imagery:** temporary stand-ins pre-samples only; hard gate — every purchasable product
  photo must be a real photo of the real product before real ad spend.
- **What is never done:** fake reviews/social proof, urgency theater, trend/music chasing,
  "supporting Palestine" framing.

## 9. Wholesale — future channel, not a current priority

Exploratory only. The eventual shape: a simple line-sheet/catalog PDF (products, materials,
provenance story, wholesale pricing, MOQs) pitched to independent design/home-goods boutiques
and museum shops — venues whose customers already pay for provenance. Prerequisites before any
wholesale effort makes sense: retail pricing set (wholesale is typically ~50% of retail — the
margin must survive that), unit costs known, supply capacity proven beyond DTC demand, and at
least one batch's QC/logistics track record. Risks to respect: wholesale eats the thin
capacity of the artisan supply chain and concentrates payment risk in net-30/60 terms.
Revisit seriously only after DTC is stable; a catalog is cheap to produce when that day comes.

## 11. Phased roadmap & milestones

**Phase 0 — Foundation (now → samples in hand)**
Samples ordered from both glass sources + ceramics ✅ in motion. Meanwhile: resolve PostHog
capture; settle registration/customs questions (§2); finish honest-launch checklist items;
bank first content from AI-stand-in→real transition plan. *Exit: samples arrived, QC verdict
on each supplier, real photography begun.*

**Phase 1 — Organic warm-up (2–4 weeks)**
Post on cadence (2–3 statics/wk, 1 process video/1–2 wks); build the feed strangers will land
on. Set pricing (unblocked by sample landed costs). *Exit: 3–4 weeks of banked content, feed
live, pricing decided, store technically launch-ready.*

**Phase 2 — Paid validation (2–3 weeks, $10–20/day)**
Launch store quietly (password off). Run 2–3 creatives to cold audiences; judge on funnel
shape per MARKETING_STRATEGY.md §5. *Exit: scale signals present (→ Phase 3) or absent
(→ iterate price/creative/product and re-run once; capital allows ~two loops).*

**Phase 3 — First real batch**
Order small batch from the sample-validated supplier(s) with reserve held back; optionally
open honest pre-orders per 6.4 conditions; fulfill from home; collect the first genuine
reviews and reshoot content with production pieces. *Exit: first batch sold through, real
unit economics known, breakage rate known.*

**Phase 4 — Compounding (quarter-scale horizon)**
Reorder cadence established; margin-funded ad scaling (≤20–30%/wk); evaluate second ceramics
supplier; begin research on a second craft origin (the first true "Collective" expansion);
revisit AR locale, newsletter backend, and wholesale only when each has a real justification.

*No calendar dates are attached beyond Phase 0 — the phases gate on evidence, not the
calendar, and supplier logistics from the West Bank make date promises dishonest even
internally.*
